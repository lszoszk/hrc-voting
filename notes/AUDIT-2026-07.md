# hrc-voting — audit: user experience & methodological soundness

> **Status: all findings below were fixed on branch `audit-fixes` (2026-07-26).**
> See `notes/AUDIT-2026-07-FIXES.md` for what changed, the measured before/after, and
> the three findings that only surfaced during the fix work. `smoke_test.py`,
> `audit.py` and `export_test.py` all pass with zero console errors.

Audited 2026-07-25 against `main` @ 9484b20. Dashboard served from `dashboard/` at
1280×720 and 375×812; data checks run directly against `data/csv/*`, `dashboard/data.js`
and `dashboard/texts/*`. No console errors on any tab; no horizontal overflow at either
viewport.

Findings are ordered by how much they could change a reader's conclusion, not by effort
to fix. Every number below is reproducible from the repo.

---

## Summary

The pipeline is sound and the Methodology tab is unusually honest — it names chance
agreement (Häge 2011), consensus-inclusion (Häge & Hug 2016), selection into roll-calls,
and the GA→HRC transfer problem, and it separates "properties of the measures" from
"evidence from this body". That is better practice than most published UN-voting work.

The problems cluster in one place: **four measures are documented as one thing and
implemented as another**, and the two newest tabs (Consensus, Language) surface their
least reliable results most prominently. Nothing here is a parsing bug — the harvest and
MARC parsing check out.

## A. Scientific precision / methodological soundness

### A1 — "Most often outvoted" measures the *modal* side, not the winning side · HIGH

Methodology tab, §08 states: *"the share of a state's votes that differ from the majority
position (**the winning** Yes/No/Abstain side, from official whole-chamber totals)"*.

The implementation (`index.html:1715`) takes `max(Y, N, A)`:

```js
RES.forEach((r,i)=>{ ... const mx=Math.max(r.y,r.n,r.a);
  maj[i]=mx===r.y?'Y':(mx===r.n?'N':'A');});
```

`max(Y,N,A)` is the modal position, which is not the outcome. Measured over all 1,705
recorded votes:

| | count |
|---|---|
| Abstain is the modal position | 70 |
| …of which the text was nonetheless **carried** (Y > N) | **53** |
| Winning side is only a plurality of Y+N+A, not a majority | 433 (25.4%) |
| Exact ties for top position, resolved silently by Y > N > A order | 20 |

In those 53 resolutions every state that voted **Yes — the winning side —** is scored as
having been outvoted. Example: `E/CN.4/RES/1988/17` (Albania, 1988) Y=15 N=11 A=17 →
adopted, but the 15 Yes votes count as "minority side".

Effect on the published ranking (2006–2026 window; 21 of 581 resolutions change):

| as shipped | if "majority" = adoption outcome |
|---|---|
| 1 Canada 89% | 1 Canada 87% |
| 2 Bosnia 73% | 2 Bosnia 75% |
| 3 United States 68% | 3 **DR Congo** 66% |
| 4 Hungary 61% | 4 United States 66% |
| 5 DR Congo 61% | 5 Moldova 59% |
| 6 United Kingdom 59% | … UK falls to **#9** |

The mislabel travels: the CSV export column is `minority_side_pct` under the title
"vote against the majority".

**Fix** — either change the code to `y>n?'Y':(n>y?'N':'A')`, or change the wording in
§08, the chart caption and the CSV header to "the most common position". These are
different measures and both are defensible; only one matches the current text.

### A2 — "Not a member" markers are inferred from silence, and are wrong for the whole CHR era · HIGH

Country profile, chart 01 caption: *"Dashed gaps: years outside CHR/HRC membership."*
Tooltip (`index.html:908`): `'Not a member '+(ys[i].y+1)+…`. The gap is drawn whenever a
state's roll-call years are non-consecutive.

**12 calendar years contain zero recorded votes for any state**: 1949, 1951, 1953, 1956,
1957, 1959–1963, 1965, 1973. Nine more have only 1–2. In those years the dashboard tells
every user that every state was not a member of a body that was in fact meeting.

Verified output for four permanent fixtures of the Commission:

```
FRA  "Not a member 1949" · "1951" · "1953" · "1956–1957" · "1959–1963" · "1965" · "1973" · "1977"
GBR  "Not a member 1949" · "1951" · "1953" · "1956–1957" · "1959–1963" · "1965" · "1973" · "1979" · "1991"
CHN  "Not a member 1959–1981"
```

France and the United Kingdom sat on the Commission continuously from 1947 to 2006.
The HRC-era markers are broadly correct (non-members genuinely cannot vote, so
USA "2006–2008" and "2019–2021" are real), which makes the CHR-era ones more dangerous —
the feature looks reliable where it is checkable.

**Fix** — the honest label is what the data supports: *"no recorded vote cast"*. Suppress
the marker entirely for years in which the chamber recorded no roll-call at all, or
restrict the "membership" reading to ≥2006.

### A3 — The Language tab draws an 80-year axis over a 33-year corpus · HIGH

`dashboard/texts/catalog.json` holds 4,437 documents spanning **1993–2026** — the AP
mirror starts at CHR 1993. `lang.json.byYear` has 34 points, first `{"y":1993}`.

The Texts tab handles this correctly and prominently: *"4,437 texts · 1993–2026 · more
being added"*, and its year selectors start at 1993.

The Language tab, built from the same corpus, does not. Its chart renders x-axis ticks at
**1950, 1960, 1970, 1980, 1990**, 2000, 2010, 2020 with 34 plotted points, leaving the
left 55% of the plot blank and unexplained. Worse, the headline reads
**"Commission 3.4 → Council 3.5"** — "Commission" here means CHR 1993–2005 only, i.e. the
last 13 of its 60 years, but the page masthead says "Commission on Human Rights
(1946–2006)".

**Fix** — clamp the axis to the corpus range, and change the headline to
"Commission (1993–2005) 3.4 → Council 3.5". Add the corpus range to Methodology §11.

### A4 — The "delegation" flag counts mentions, not machinery · HIGH

Methodology §11: *"A third flag, delegation, marks clauses that **create machinery** (a
mandate, report or inquiry)."* The scatter's y-axis is labelled
*"creates machinery (delegation) →"*.

`tag_terms.py:75` matches any occurrence anywhere in the clause of `secretary-general |
high commissioner | ohchr | special rapporteur | independent expert | working group |
commission of inquiry | fact-finding | to (report|submit|present|prepare|convene|
establish|appoint) | the mandate | a panel | a report`.

**17,833 of 44,466 operative clauses (40.1%) are flagged.** The top matches are bare
mentions:

| matches | trigger | actual clause |
|---|---|---|
| 5,612 | `high commissioner` | "*Decides to include in the provisional agenda of its fiftieth session…*" |
| 3,604 | `special rapporteur` | "*Expresses its appreciation for the preliminary report…*" |
| 997 | `independent expert` | "*Expresses its satisfaction with the study by the independent expert…*" |
| 137 | `fact-finding` | "*Urges the Government of Papua New Guinea to permit international fact-finding missions access…*" |

None of these create machinery. Two of them create nothing at all. And because OHCHR was
established in 1993 and the special-procedures system expanded steadily after, the
mention rate carries a built-in upward time trend that has nothing to do with drafting
behaviour — precisely the confound the axis is meant to measure.

**Fix** — require the verb *and* the delegation object in the same clause: restrict to
directive tiers D3–D5 and require an infinitival task (`to report|submit|prepare|
establish|convene|appoint`) or an explicit `Establishes|Renews|Extends … mandate`. Then
re-label the axis honestly. Expect the share to fall well below 40%.

### A5 — `Demands` is in two directive tiers; the UI contradicts itself on screen · MED-HIGH

`tag_terms.py:52-57` lists `"Demands"` in both `DIR[5]` and `DIR[4]`. The dict
comprehension at line 70 resolves it by insertion order, so **`DIR_OF['Demands'] == 4`** —
silently, and not by any recorded decision.

The consequence is visible in the Language tab without any digging. Section 02 renders:

```
D5 · DECISIONAL — DECIDES / DEMANDS / ESTABLISHES     ← header names Demands
    Decides 3,618 · Renews 50 · Declares 43 · …       ← Demands is absent
D4 · STRONG EXHORTATION — URGES / CALLS UPON
    Calls upon 4,603 · Urges 3,438 · Demands 389 · …  ← here it is
```

Methodology §11 repeats the D5 claim: *"D5 decisional (Decides, **Demands**, Establishes)"*.

The same defect hits the sentiment axis: the header reads
**"−2 · CONDEMNATION — CONDEMNS / DENOUNCES"** while `Denounces` is coded −1 and renders
under "−1 · DISAPPROVAL".

**Fix** — delete `"Demands"` from one tier (D5 is the better reading — *demands* is a
decisional act, not exhortation) and drop `Denounces` from the −2 header. Two-line change;
removes two on-screen contradictions.

### A6 — Topic trend notes publish directional claims with no n and no significance · MED

`trendData` (`index.html:1518`) fires whenever there are ≥3 year-points spanning ≥5 years,
and prints "rising"/"falling" on `|slope| ≥ 2 pp/decade`. There is no fit quality, no
sample size, no uncertainty. The regression runs on **yearly means, unweighted**, so a
year with one resolution has the same leverage as a year with four.

Ordinary-least-squares diagnostics for shipped topics:

| subject | n res | year pts | shown | R² | t | sig .05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Capital punishment | 17 | 17 | "rising +4.9" | 0.64 | 5.11 | ✅ |
| Democracy and human rights | 27 | 19 | "falling −7.7" | 0.43 | −3.56 | ✅ |
| Right to development | 31 | 29 | "falling −4.6" | 0.14 | −2.12 | borderline |
| Self-determination | 25 | 13 | "rising +3.4" | 0.26 | 1.95 | ❌ |
| **Human rights defenders** | **4** | **3** | **"falling −7.7"** | 0.48 | −0.97 | ❌ |

The last row is a directional claim about a politically live topic drawn from three
points. Capital punishment, by contrast, is genuinely robust — the feature works, it just
has no floor.

**Fix** — require ≥8 year-points and |t| > 2 before printing a direction; otherwise print
"no clear trend". Append `(n = X resolutions over Y years)` to the note either way.

### A7 — The Consensus tab's hero panel ranks its least reliable entries first · MED

`flipStats` needs ≥6 resolutions in a subject and |Δ| ≥ 40 pp between the first and second
half. The halves are split by **rank position, not by time** (`rows.slice(0,half)`), so a
"before/after" comparison can straddle an arbitrary gap.

Of 33 flips: **15 have fewer than 10 resolutions**, and **14 have halves whose time spans
differ by ≥10 years**. Sorting is by |Δ|, so the sparsest cases float to the top:

```
POLAND        n=6   0%→100%  [1982-2017]  rapprochement
TIMOR-LESTE   n=8   0%→100%  [1983-2017]  rapprochement
CHINA         n=6   0%→100%  [1990-2019]  rapprochement
NICARAGUA     n=9  75%→  0%  [1979-2025]  breakdown
```

"CHINA — rapprochement, 0%→100%" reads as *the Council stopped needing to vote on China*.
The actual history is the opposite: contested China resolutions were repeatedly defeated
by no-action motions in the 1990s and then stopped being tabled at all. Six items spread
over 29 years is not a before/after.

Methodology §09 does say small series *"should be read as candidates for investigation,
not findings"* — but the panel's own sort order guarantees that the entries the caveat
disowns are the ones a reader sees first. Country names as flip subjects also make these
the most quotable and the most misquotable items on the page.

**Fix** — sort by a reliability-weighted score (e.g. Δ × √n) rather than raw Δ; require
both halves to span ≥5 years each; render n < 10 rows in a visually demoted "candidates"
group.

### A8 — The UNITAR vocabulary is described as larger than the one implemented · MED

Methodology §11: *"the UNITAR glossary Terms Used in Resolutions (JG-1/02/10 — **103
operative verbs, 56 preambular openers**)"*.

Implemented: `len(OPERATIVE) == 77`, `len(PREAMBLE) == 49`.

This matters beyond bookkeeping. The tab's closing finding — *"the corpus uses operative
verbs absent from the 2010 UNITAR list, above all Encourages (~3,100 clauses)"* — is only
sound if the reference list is complete. With 26 operative terms unaccounted for, a
"beyond the glossary" claim cannot be distinguished from a transcription gap.

Compounding it, `build_lang`'s `track_new` only counts heads against a hard-coded
whitelist (`tag_terms.py:158`) that already contains `"encourages"`, so the panel is
structurally incapable of discovering a verb its author did not anticipate. (The
unrestricted counter in `main()` writes to `terms.json`, which the dashboard does not
read.)

**Fix** — reconcile the transcribed list against JG-1/02/10 and correct whichever number
is wrong; drive the "beyond the glossary" panel from the unrestricted counter.

### A9 — Thematic / country-situation split misclassifies 12 subject tags · MED

`is_country_subject` (`build_dashboard_data.py:192`) matches subject strings against the
names of **states that cast roll-call votes**. Territories and non-member states are
therefore invisible to it. Confirmed misclassifications, all shown to users as "Thematic":

```
 32  GOLAN HEIGHTS                    ← 4th-largest "thematic" tag in the dataset
 10  SELF-DETERMINATION - PALESTINIANS
  6  SOUTH SUDAN (2 tags)
  6  MYANMAR (3 tags)
  4  TIMOR-LESTE
  2  YEMEN
  1  XIZANG (CHINA) · DARFUR (SUDAN) · PALESTINIANS - REFUGEES
```

Methodology §05 already flags the heuristic as *"imperfect at the margins"*, which is fair
— but Golan Heights at 32 resolutions is not a margin, and the Topics tab exposes
Thematic/Country as a first-class filter.

**Fix** — add a small explicit override list to `build_dashboard_data.py` for territories
and non-voting states. Ten lines, and it makes the filter trustworthy.

### A10 — `Expresses` is scored two different ways in two panels of the same tab · MED

`code_clause` resolves the valence of `Expresses`/`Notes`/`Takes note`/`Recognizes`
per-clause from the object ("*expresses grave concern*" → −1, "*expresses appreciation*"
→ +1). The vocabulary chart, however, is built from
`VAL_OF.get(v, 0)` (`tag_terms.py:225`) and files **`Expresses` (1,885 clauses)** flat
under *"0 · NEUTRAL REGISTERING"*.

`Expresses` is the largest expressive verb in the corpus, so the two panels disagree about
the single biggest contributor to the sentiment axis.

**Fix** — either split the bar into its resolved −1/0/+1 components, or annotate it
"valence resolved per clause".

### A11 — Uncoded verbs default to "neutral" rather than "unknown" · MED-LOW

`tag_terms.py:141`: `val = None if d else VAL_OF.get(v, 0)`. A verb that is matched but
carries no valence code is scored **0**, i.e. treated as deliberate neutrality. Affected
UNITAR terms: `Calls the attention`, `Calls attention`, `Draws the attention`,
`Invites the attention`, `Reminds`. Same pattern in `bySubj`, where a subject with no
valenced clauses is emitted as `0` rather than null (`tag_terms.py:212`).

50.5% of all valence-coded clauses already sit at exactly 0, so the axis is heavily
concentrated at the midpoint before any defaulting is added.

**Fix** — return `None` for uncoded verbs and drop them from the mean.

### A12 — 2026 is a partial year and no time axis says so · MED-LOW

2026 holds 44 catalogued resolutions against 138 in 2025 and 208 in 2023. Every time
series in the app — overview volume, consensus share, language metrics, topic trends —
ends on a collapsing final point that reads as a substantive decline. The Language tab's
final year is built from 29 resolutions.

**Fix** — hatch or annotate the current year ("2026 — year in progress"), and exclude it
from every fitted slope.

### A13 — "Most often outvoted" mixes exposures that differ sevenfold, unranked by n · MED-LOW

The threshold is ≥20 votes in the window. In 2006–2026 the ranking is topped by
**Canada at 89% on 62 votes**, ahead of the **United Kingdom at 59% on 433**. A 62-vote
proportion has roughly 2.6× the standard error of a 433-vote one, and the chart ranks them
on the point estimate alone; n appears only in the hover tooltip.

**Fix** — raise the floor to ~50 votes, or draw a Wilson interval on each bar. The
existing caveat — *"being on the losing side is not the same as being isolated"* — is good
and should stay.

### A14 — Minor data-integrity items · LOW

- **CHN conflates two representations.** ISO `CHN` spans the Republic of China (to Oct
  1971) and the PRC thereafter as one continuous "China" series, with no break marker —
  in a dashboard that otherwise marks the 2006 institutional break on every axis.
- **One roll-call row is silently dropped.** Record 24737 (`E/CN.4/RES/6(XX)`, 1964) has a
  malformed MARC 967 with no `$b`/`$e`. `build_dashboard_data.py:145` skips it. Hence
  README says 80,159 roll-calls and the app says 80,158. Harmless, but it is a published
  figure in both places.
- **Reconciliation stands at 148/1,705 (8.7%)**, matching the README's "~148 (~9%)".
  Correctly disclosed in Methodology §06. No action needed.

### A15 — README documents six views; eight ship · LOW

`README.md:39` — *"Six views"* — then lists Overview, Country, Topics, Blocs, Consensus,
Methodology. **Texts** and **Language** are undocumented, including in the Layout section
(no mention of `build_text_index.py`, `tag_terms.py`, `harvest_ods_texts.py`,
`mirror_ap_ohchr.py`, `data/ap_mirror/`, `data/ods_texts/`, `dashboard/texts/`). The
first-run Tour has the same gap: its view list stops at "06 Texts".

Given the Zenodo DOI and CITATION.cff, the README is the citable description of the
software. It should cover what ships.

## B. User experience

### B1 — On mobile, 57% of the viewport is fixed chrome · HIGH

Measured at 375×812 (`.tabs` and `.foot` are fixed grid areas; only `.main` scrolls):

| region | height | share |
|---|---|---|
| masthead | 188 px | 23% |
| tab rows (8 tabs wrapping to 3 lines) + Scope | 129 px | 16% |
| **scrollable content** | **347 px** | **43%** |
| footer | 139 px | 17% |

The footer alone takes 139 px to render a citation line whose link text
("Source: OHCHR Search Library — Voting collection") wraps across five lines, plus four
palette buttons and a tour button. A phone user reads dense charts through a 347-pixel
slot.

**Fix** — collapse the footer to a single truncated line on `≤600px` (or move it behind an
"ⓘ" affordance); make the masthead compact on scroll; move the palette switcher into the
Methodology tab. Recovering ~200px would grow the content area by 58%.

### B2 — The headline numbers ignore the Scope filter and contradict the tile below them · HIGH

With the default Scope = "Resolutions only":

```
masthead (top right):   1,705 recorded · 80,158 roll-calls
tile (directly below):  1,248 recorded resolutions · 58,690 roll-call votes cast
footer:                 1,705 recorded of 6,346 catalogued resolutions
```

Both figures are correct for their own scope, but the masthead and footer are static
(`hd-stat` is never re-rendered by `switchTab`/scope change) while the tiles are scoped.
The most prominent number on the page disagrees with the number 200 pixels beneath it, and
the discrepancy is unexplained. The Tour repeats the unscoped 1,705.

**Fix** — recompute `hd-stat` and the footer count on scope change, or append "· all
scopes" to them.

### B3 — Every acronym in a subject tag is mangled · MED

`tc()` (`index.html:1250`) lowercases then re-capitalises, with no acronym guard. This
runs on subject tags across Topics chips, Consensus rows, Language rankings, tooltips and
CSV titles:

```
OHCHR                       → Ohchr
HIV/AIDS                    → Hiv/Aids
UNESCO                      → Unesco
UN. Human Rights Council    → Un. Human Rights Council
ILO - CHILD LABOUR          → Ilo - Child Labour
USA - CUBA                  → Usa - Cuba
```

"Un. Human Rights Council" is currently the top row of the Language tab's "most directive
topics" ranking. The README's own showcase example — HIV/AIDS 94%→0% — renders "Hiv/Aids".
On a page this carefully typeset it reads as a defect.

**Fix** — preserve any input token that is all-caps and ≥2 characters.

### B4 — The footer is clipped at 1280px · MED

`.fl` measures `scrollWidth 1030` against `clientWidth 829`, so the line truncates
mid-word: *"…1947–2026 · buj"* (from "built with a reproducible pipeline"). `.foot` sets
`overflow:hidden; white-space:nowrap` with no ellipsis on the flex child. Visible at the
most common laptop width.

**Fix** — `text-overflow: ellipsis` is already on `.fl`; it does not apply because the
child spans overflow it. Truncate the source-link span instead, or drop "built with a
reproducible pipeline" below 1440px.

### B5 — The consent banner sits on top of live content · MED

On first load at 1280×720 the GA banner overlays the bottom-right of the "Recorded votes
per year" chart (obscuring the 2010–2020 x-axis labels) and the header row of the "Most
divided votes" table. The Tour already defers to it (commit 193f2d4); the banner itself
does not reserve space.

**Fix** — reserve layout space (push `.main` up by the banner height) rather than
overlaying, or anchor it to the footer strip.

### B6 — Country display names carry title-case artefacts into exports · MED

`build_dashboard_data.py:156` applies Python `.title()`; `NAME_FIX` patches only 7 ISO
codes. Surviving:

```
BIH  Bosnia And Herzegovina      CIV  Cote D'Ivoire
GNB  Guinea Bissau               KOR  Republic Of Korea
MDA  Republic Of Moldova         STP  Sao Tome And Principe
```

These appear in the country picker, chart labels, tooltips **and every CSV export** —
verified in `hrc-most-outvoted-2006-2026.csv`. "Cote D'Ivoire" also drops the diacritic.

**Fix** — extend `NAME_FIX` with these six, and lowercase the joining words in the Python
titling step to match the JS `tc()` behaviour.

### B7 — The first-run Tour is a version behind · MED

Its view list runs "01 Overview … 06 Texts" and stops; Language and Methodology are
missing. A first-time visitor is introduced to six of eight tabs. Same root cause as A15.

### B8 — Alignment map: unexplained drop-outs and colliding labels · LOW

With Country set = "30 most active states", the note reads
*"all 110 states · **28** highlighted"*. Two of the 30 fail the `co < 20` shared-vote
guard at `index.html:1691` and vanish without explanation. At the top-left of the plot
`RUS`, `CUB` and `VEN` labels overlap into an unreadable stack.

**Fix** — say "28 of 30 shown (2 below the 20-shared-vote threshold)"; add simple label
collision avoidance or leader lines.

### B9 — Historical states sit unmarked beside their successors · LOW

The country picker lists `Czechoslovakia` next to `Czech Republic`, and `Germany`,
`Germany (FRG)` and `Germany (GDR)` as three peers. Only the German trio is disambiguated
(via `NAME_FIX`); `Czechoslovakia`, `Yugoslavia`, `USSR` and `Zaire` are not marked as
historical, and nothing indicates their date ranges before selection.

**Fix** — append the active span to historical entries, e.g. "Czechoslovakia (1947–1992)",
or group them under an "Historical states" optgroup.

### B10 — Duplicated clause markers in Texts results · LOW

Sub-item clauses render their label and then repeat it from the clause text:
`OP2(e)(e) To adopt a victim and survivor-centred…`. Cosmetic; `segment()` keeps the
`(e)` prefix in the clause body while `renderTexts` also prepends the derived label.

## What is working well

Worth preserving through any fix:

- **Provenance.** Every table row links to its OHCHR record; every CSV carries a BOM, the
  chart title, the active scope, the full source citation and an access date. This is
  better than most published research artefacts.
- **The amendments Scope filter.** Defaulting to resolutions-only, with the Fjelstul/Hug/
  Kilby 2025 justification and two agreeing catalogue signals for detection, is a real
  methodological contribution — most UN-voting datasets get this wrong silently.
- **`meta.coverage` computed at build time.** The Methodology tab's numbers cannot drift
  from the CSVs. This is why the tab is trustworthy where the prose descriptions are not.
- **The Agreement Index is implemented correctly.** `(mx − 0.5·(tot − mx)) / tot` matches
  Hix–Noury–Roland 2005 exactly, over a documented ≥3-votes-per-group floor.
- **Caveats that cost something.** Naming Häge 2011 on chance agreement and Häge & Hug
  2016 on consensus inclusion undercuts the tab's own headline numbers. Keep that.
- **Responsive discipline.** Zero horizontal overflow and zero console errors across all
  eight tabs at both viewports.
- **The Texts tab discloses its corpus range correctly** — which is exactly what the
  Language tab needs to copy (A3).

## Suggested order of work

1. **A1, A2, A4** — three measures whose documented meaning differs from their
   implementation. Each is either a one-line code change or a one-line wording change;
   what matters is that code and prose agree.
2. **A5, B2, B3** — visible self-contradictions on screen. Small diffs, disproportionate
   credibility gain.
3. **A3, A15, B7** — corpus-range and version drift in Language / README / Tour.
4. **B1** — mobile chrome budget. The largest UX win, and the largest layout change.
5. **A6, A7, A13** — put a statistical floor under the three panels that publish
   directional claims.
6. Everything else.
